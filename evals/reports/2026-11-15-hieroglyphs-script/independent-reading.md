- The invented Alexander sound sequence does not match the name it is supposed
  to explain. The ordinary reader correctly requests a supported, coherent
  illustration or a simpler account of testing subsequent names.
- Two adjacent passages give de Sacy incompatible positions: he recognizes
  that demotic has too many signs for a simple alphabet, then is grouped with
  Akerblad as assuming that every sign is a sound. The sequence needs a clear
  account of whose belief applied when. The ordinary reader did not flag this.
- The account of Young's inference from the shape of demotic signs needs careful
  attribution. Retained S3 explicitly describes this as Young's reasoning; it
  must not be silently replaced with an invented historical argument. The
  ordinary reader's requested change leaves room to clarify the attribution.
- The passage about interchangeable signs declares equal sound before explaining
  what the independently established sound table contributes. The ordinary reader
  correctly identifies the missing link.
- The modern explanation connects omitted vowels to `Рамсес/Рамзес`, although
  that displayed difference is a consonant. This is a concrete explanatory error,
  not a reason to add a general warning about ancient pronunciation.
- The refinement from provisional `m` to `ms` is distinguished from the original
  hypothesis, but its relation to the remaining `s` signs is left unclear. Check
  whether the final telling remains intelligible without expanding into an

  again around the birth comparison. A precise attribution can carry a connected

  researchers and materials again. Its broad consequence is useful; the repeated
  route through the discoveries is less useful. The ordinary reader accepts the
  ending as a whole.

## Reading after both permitted revisions

Root and an independent reviewer read `revise-2-attempt-0.md` in full. The first
revision implemented the seven assigned repairs: it attributes Young's reasoning
explicitly, distinguishes his reading of the lion from Champollion's, replaces
the incoherent Alexander example, explains the comparison of interchangeable
signs and removes two repeated explanations. The ordinary reader then noticed
the previously missed consonant/vowel error. The second revision changes only
that example, replacing `Рамсес/Рамзес` with `Ра/ре`.

The complete account has a coherent progression. Comparing a second name,
testing older royal names and moving to an ordinary word each answer a different
question. The topic has not simply been stretched by repeating the same lesson.
The rewritten Alexander demonstration is more demanding to follow in audio, but
it now has a traceable structure. The ending's short list of contributors can
reasonably collect the different contributions into a broad conclusion;
preferring a shorter version is not by itself a defect requiring another rewrite.

The strongest remaining problems are unchanged passages:

1. De Sacy first recognizes that there are too many demotic signs for a simple
   alphabet, then is described as assuming a wholly alphabetic system. The next
   passage reinforces that explanation of his failure. The script does not
   establish a change of belief or otherwise reconcile the two descriptions.
2. The attribution passage discloses Ramesses before the narrator invites the
   listener to discover that name from the signs. This weakens the discovery
   the plan deliberately sets up.
3. The narrator repeatedly interrupts the September story to comment on the
   reliability and presentation of the account. Initial attribution is useful;
   subsequent comments about a non-verbatim chronology and the narrator's
   explanatory order often repeat that work. The ordinary reader explicitly
   retained and praised these passages, so their survival is also an editorial
   decision, not merely an unexecuted author instruction.

The transition from provisional `m` to modern `ms` also leaves an explanatory
question about the following `s` signs. It should be assessed as a clarity issue,
not treated as proof that the historical reading is false or as a demand for a
complete Egyptology lesson. These observations were not inserted into Podgen's
inputs and the generated text was not edited by hand.
