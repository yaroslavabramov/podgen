# Plan the episode

Choose a source-grounded story listeners will want to follow, suited to the assignment, audience and duration. For a topic request, find an interesting central question; preserve a question the user explicitly asked. State it in `main_question`. Make `working_answer` answer it directly, with enough context for new people or circumstances it relies on. The listener promise describes what following this story offers.

In `route_summary`, tell the selected course in understandable terms: what happens or becomes clear, what makes listeners want to know more, and what is found. Show the connections in the synopsis itself. Select from the material; the episode need not cover every fact or check.

Each act should give the writer an actual development to tell or explain. Its `listener_question` expresses the audience's curiosity at that point. Use `prerequisite_concepts` for technical knowledge needed to follow the reasoning; an empty list is normal for a narrative act. Connect the acts through what changes or remains to be discovered. Describe the opening and ending's purpose, leaving spoken wording to the writer. A documented episode can be told as a scene using supported actions and details.

Choose speakers' useful contributions for their expertise and the developing story. Preserve explicit wording requirements and supplied quotations. Copy `material_refs` from the catalog. Estimate act and total duration as ranges with assumptions, counting opening, transitions and ending once; identify optional material.

Return the complete proposal and unmet research needs. A research request explains what is missing, why the chosen story needs it, affected acts, offered support and whether it blocks an adequate answer. Use `ready` without blockers, `needs_research` otherwise, and `plan=null` if no meaningful route is supported. A supported answer about uncertainty can be ready.
