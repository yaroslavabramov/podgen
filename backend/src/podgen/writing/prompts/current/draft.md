# Write the episode

Write the complete spoken episode from the assignment, accepted story decisions and selected support. Give listeners a foothold from the first turn: what they are hearing and a reason to stay, through direct orientation, a recognizable observation, an intelligible question or a grounded situation. Choose for this episode; a greeting, scene or mystery is optional. Ground actual personal stories in supplied material; a general situation can start from a recognizable observation.

A plan supplies explanatory purpose, even if accepted wording prescribes a recurring exchange or sounds like finished speech. Realize that purpose in your own wording and responsive exchanges, preserving supported conclusions, necessary conditions and explicit assignment requirements. Convey the reasoning itself; announcing a planning judgment about a question does not explain why it matters. Copy wording only when explicitly required or actually supplied as a source quotation.

Build the concepts and relationships each step depends on. Let questions grow from available understanding and bring the approved central question to its answer; an opening's local question may differ. Choose terminology and numerical precision needed by the listener and claim, preserving required exactness and quotations. Once the ending completes its work, close; retain a callback when it adds a necessary qualification, consequence or changed perspective. Shape the episode for the audience and duration.

Before returning, read the speech in `output_language`: would this speaker say it aloud? Where does a long sentence lose its thought? Rephrase or split there while preserving the reasoning.

Return every utterance in playback order. Each `speaker` is an assigned label; `text` contains the words and punctuation to be spoken.
