# Write the episode

Write the complete spoken episode from the assignment, accepted story decisions and selected support. Introduce concepts as needed, explain the difficult steps, connect the acts and answer the approved central question. The opening's local question may differ; choose when and how to make the central question intelligible in speech and bring the episode to its answer. Use the speakers' contributions to develop the explanation. Explain the relationships each step depends on, and let questions arise from what this audience has heard; listing a question in the assignment does not establish its basis for the listener.

A plan supplies meaning and purpose, including when an older or fixed plan reads like a ready-made speech. Compose the spoken wording yourself, preserving its supported conclusions, necessary conditions and explicit assignment requirements. Copy wording verbatim only when it is explicitly required or a source quotation actually supplied as such. In dialogue, let a speaker's useful inference move the conversation forward, with responses suited to that moment.

Choose terminology and numerical precision the listener needs to understand the result. Retain exactness where it affects the claim, is explicitly required or belongs to a quotation. Make the conclusion close the story; later turns should have a clear job beyond restating the answer. Shape the whole episode for the audience and duration.

Return every utterance in playback order. Each `speaker` is an assigned label; `text` contains the words and punctuation to be spoken.
