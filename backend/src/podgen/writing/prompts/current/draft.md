# Write a complete podcast people want to hear

Write for a person listening once, in `output_language`. Use the assignment, accepted story and factual material. Give the assigned speakers a voice and the subject a tangible presence. The listener should follow the account and enjoy how it is told.

## Compose the experience

Make the opening's interest audible from what listeners know at that point. Establish the person or situation that makes a question matter. Compose the order inside each discovery-led scene too: let listeners encounter the situation and clues before explaining the connection they are meant to discover. Check the first spoken disclosure across earlier previews and both sides of transitions; a later rearrangement cannot undo an answer already given. Plan headings and the full synopsis inform you without having to become speech. A source summary's order need not be the spoken order. A teaser can name an object or outcome while saving a worthwhile discovery. During a delayed answer, develop that expectation through discoveries, changing possibilities or other worthwhile moments.

Honor the client's explicit question and requirements. Use `story.route_summary` to understand the proposed course. The plan's central question, promise, order and selection are working decisions you may revise to tell this episode well. Preserve supported meaning while preparing an unearned question, moving background or rebuilding a detour. If a necessary premise is missing, choose an entry the evidence supports rather than inventing a motivation. Select a complete telling instead of voicing every supporting category.

Let actions and consequences carry a narrative; let an idea explain something listeners can picture in an explainer. Reflection can add a particular thought, feeling or change of scale. Choose the balance for this episode. End when its promise has landed, allowing a final image or thought room to work.

## Write with a voice

Imagine this speaker talking to someone they want to keep listening. Use idiomatic phrasing in the episode's language, tangible nouns and active verbs. Let a thought be held on first hearing. Vary sentence and turn length, with room for a response or image to land. In dialogue, contributions should affect what follows and fit each speaker's knowledge.

Choose details for presence, character, feeling or pleasure as well as explanation. A playful observation can earn its place without advancing a proof. Humor, previews, direct address, asides and callbacks are available choices. Judge their timing and attitude. A dry joke can land through understatement. Let reflection say something about the subject rather than certify the author's care or answer an objection the account has not raised.

When `craft_reference` is supplied, study how its revised passages select material, reveal a connection, clarify a necessary point or leave a scene to land. Its fictional material belongs to those examples. Make your own decisions from this episode's evidence and write in `output_language`.

## Keep the selected account accurate

Introduce names, terms and numbers where they help listeners understand what is happening. Select the comparisons the episode needs. If an unnecessary exact value needs a long detour, omit it and adjust dependent comparisons. Keep the meaning of retained quantities accurate. Available evidence need not become a spoken inventory.

Keep established events, causal grounds and meaningful uncertainty accurate. Repair a misleading claim through wording, scope, attribution or placement, or remove a dispensable thought. Source cautions guide what can be said; they do not all need to be said themselves. Use accurate attribution and temporal framing where they settle the issue. Add explanation when the retained account still needs it or the client requires it. A separate warning needs an actual misleading claim or implication in this telling. Let listeners connect details when the connection is already clear. Reflection should add a thought of its own. After moving events, recheck what each person knew at that time and rebuild the surrounding composition, not just its dates.

Scenic reconstruction, original analogies and imaginative texture may enliven the telling within the brief. They cannot manufacture substantive evidence, change established events or present invented words as source testimony. Source summaries are paraphrases; direct source quotations require the supplied wording.

## Finish the script

Read the whole result as speech. Repair stiff phrasing, repeated sentence patterns, delayed orientation and stalled sequences. Use the assignment's duration target and bounds to guide selection and pacing, keeping texture and breathing room. Return every utterance in playback order in the requested structure, with exact assigned speaker labels and only spoken words and punctuation in `text`.
