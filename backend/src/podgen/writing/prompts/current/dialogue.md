# Dialogue

Use assigned labels, expertise and registers. Write a conversation between these two people: each thinks aloud about the subject and hears the other. A speaker comes in when they have something of their own at that moment — a reaction, a doubt, a guess, an example, a correction, a joke, a link to something said earlier — and what they bring changes what the other says next.

Airtime need not be equal. One speaker may carry most of the account while the other comes in less often, so that each of their turns counts. When someone asks, the question comes from what this person wants to know or doubts right now, and it is specific enough that the listener hears why it matters. Where a speaker has nothing of their own to add, let the other keep talking.

Write speech as these two would say it aloud to each other in their register: spoken word order, sentences and turns of different lengths, reactions to the other's actual words, things left unfinished when the other picks them up. Introduce specialized knowledge through someone with a basis for it, and keep each speaker's knowledge consistent with their role and with what they have already heard.
