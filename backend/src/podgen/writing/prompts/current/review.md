# Review the spoken episode

First consider the opening's spoken words alone: a title, brief, source or familiar retelling has not been heard. Then read every turn consecutively from the audience's stated knowledge. Reconstruct the question, answer and reasoning, identifying premises used before being established. Compare with the assignment and factual conditions. This prefix check is within an informed review; report `reading_mode=informed` because the task frame and support are supplied.

Assess the opening's foothold and reason to listen, and the whole episode's movement in the assigned genre. For an investigation, check how documented observations, tests and changed explanations advance the account. In dialogue, follow how contributions change responses and whether knowledge fits expertise and preceding speech. Distinguish useful orientation and acknowledgment from spoken planning judgments or recurring tests and approvals. Respect interviews and expert roles.

Track where the answer and ending complete their work. Identify what later passages add: a qualification, consequence, inference or changed perspective. Judge connected runs cumulatively: repeated summaries postponing closure without such work require consolidation. Report this as `important` and recommend `revise`, even when facts remain understandable. Apply the same principle to a dialogue pattern needing reconstruction across exchanges. `optional` means an already working sequence could be refined, not that a structural defect leaves facts intact. Preserve useful callbacks, fitting acknowledgment and precision needed by the claim, assignment or quotation; repeated words alone are not a defect.

Check claims against attributed evidence: populations, periods, units, operations, dates, source dependence and causal conditions. Keep material objections and uncertainty with affected conclusions. Check literal wording, negation and place value. State the duration estimate's basis.

Locate connected problems, explain their effect and request repairs at the needed scale, stating what to preserve. Keep priority and decision consistent with the repair. Anchor to zero-based utterance indices and exact nonempty substrings, preserving case and punctuation. Use multiple anchors for patterns and real neighbors for omissions.

Return listener answer, audible limits, strengths, findings, missing-evidence questions and duration note; lists may be empty. Recommend `keep` for ready text, `needs_material` for a specific blocking evidence gap, and `revise` for repairs possible with existing support.
