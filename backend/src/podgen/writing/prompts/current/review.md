# Review the spoken episode

First consider the opening's spoken words alone: a title, brief, source or familiar retelling has not been heard. Then read every turn consecutively from the audience's stated knowledge. Reconstruct the question, answer and reasoning, identifying premises used before being established. Compare with the assignment and factual conditions. This prefix check is within an informed review; report `reading_mode=informed` because the task frame and support are supplied.

Use `editorial_frame.route_summary` to understand the proposed course. The client's explicit requirements are binding; the plan's question, promise, order and selection are working choices. Judge what the actual speech offers this audience. Use `duration_context` for a text-based estimate against the target and range, allowing natural variation rather than requiring an exact word count.

Assess the opening's foothold and reason to listen, and the whole episode's movement in the assigned genre. For an investigation, check how documented observations, tests and changed explanations advance the account. In dialogue, follow how contributions change responses and whether knowledge fits expertise and preceding speech. Distinguish useful orientation and acknowledgment from spoken planning judgments or recurring tests and approvals. Respect interviews and expert roles.

Assess background where this particular story needs orientation or meaning. Concrete details and numbers can give presence, scale or stakes as well as advance reasoning.

Judge the quality of the telling as well as its recoverable meaning. Hear the cadence and idiom of the episode's language, the narrator's perspective, the images listeners can hold and the room given to developments. Track what the speech reveals within scenes as well as between them. In a discovery-led passage, locate when the connection becomes knowable and assess what the remainder still offers. A teaser may name an object or outcome while preserving another discovery. A delayed reveal needs continuing interest during the wait. Repeated fact, limitation and summary can exhaust a sequence even when each statement is accurate. Consider selecting, moving or removing redundant proof before asking for more explanation. After a factual reorder, assess the resulting composition. A substantial failure of composition or voice warrants `important` and `revise` even when the facts can be understood.

Previews, direct address, jokes, irony, reflection and comments on the telling can contribute anticipation, insight, feeling or pleasure. Judge the particular effect rather than prohibiting a device. Distinguish an aside with a recognizable attitude from an earnest precaution against a claim the episode never suggested. Request repair when repeated self-commentary or evidential reassurance displaces the subject. Preserve useful expressive details, rather than reducing the script to an efficient delivery of facts.

Track where the answer and ending complete their work. Identify what later passages add: a qualification, consequence, inference or changed perspective. Judge connected runs cumulatively: repeated summaries postponing closure without such work require consolidation. Report this as `important` and recommend `revise`, even when facts remain understandable. Apply the same principle to a dialogue pattern needing reconstruction across exchanges. `optional` means an already working sequence could be refined, not that a structural defect leaves facts intact. Preserve useful callbacks, fitting acknowledgment and precision needed by the claim, assignment or quotation; repeated words alone are not a defect.

Check the actual spoken claims and implications against attributed evidence. Distinguish contradiction from missing confirmation in an excerpt; a later or secondhand account can coexist with an earlier firsthand one. For a required factual finding, separate the misleading meaning from a proposed means of repair. Consider removing an unnecessary quantity before requiring its accounting frame. Require added context or a statistical category only when the retained account needs it for accuracy or understanding, or the client requires it. Explain that dependency. Preserve the supported contribution, not every existing number, subgroup or caveat. Source cautions are evidence boundaries, not a list of warnings to voice. Preserve meaningful uncertainty. Check scope, dates, quantities, attribution, causal links and literal wording. State the duration estimate's basis.

Locate connected problems, explain their effect and request repairs at the needed scale, stating what to preserve. Keep priority and decision consistent with the repair. Anchor to zero-based utterance indices and exact nonempty substrings, preserving case and punctuation. Use multiple anchors for patterns and real neighbors for omissions.

Return listener answer, audible limits, strengths, findings, missing-evidence questions and duration note; lists may be empty. Recommend `keep` for ready text, `needs_material` for a specific blocking evidence gap, and `revise` for repairs possible with existing support.
