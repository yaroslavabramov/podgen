## Choose the repair

Read the actual speech first. Honor explicit client requirements. Use `editorial_frame.route_summary` to understand the proposed course; it may describe an earlier arrangement. Its question, promise, order and selection remain editorial choices, not obligations to restore. Resolve the underlying problems in required findings in the current telling. Rebuild an opening, combine or move passages, or cut a weak sequence completely when that is the appropriate repair. An interesting detail alone need not save a paragraph. Preserve what the surviving account needs and make its new joins work.

In `revision_guidance`, `positive_observations` give a quotation and the reason it worked. Preserve that contribution as you recompose; praise of accuracy does not require retaining the same spoken explanation of it. Resolve the underlying problem in a finding. For a factual finding, distinguish misleading meaning from the proposed means of repair. Its `requested_change` proposes a means, and `preserve` identifies useful contributions rather than making every listed detail mandatory. Explicit client requirements still apply. Optional suggestions are choices.

When both correction and removal can resolve a dispensable claim, compare their effect on the passage. What does keeping it give the listener: necessary reasoning, scale, stakes, character or pleasure? What attention does its accurate framing cost: new categories, dates, repetition or an interruption of the live action? Choose the repair whose contribution earns that cost in this position. Precision can itself be the discovery or carry the stakes; deletion is not the default. Judge economy by the resulting listening experience, not by how many original words survive. Recheck dependent comparisons and calculations after either choice.

In `targeted` mode, `revision_operation.targets` are permitted locations, not a checklist. Choose replacement, deletion or insertion for what the repaired passage needs. Read each proposed edit with its unchanged neighbors; a technically local replacement can still overload a scene or repeat an explanation elsewhere.

Make an opening meaningful from what listeners know then. If a necessary premise is missing, choose a supported entry or keep the specific research need explicit. Do not invent a motivation to connect the facts. Track discovery within scenes as well as across the episode: when does the connection first become knowable, including earlier previews and transitions, and what does the rest offer? Restore the encounter with clues where an early explanation has spent it, repairing the first disclosure and its dependent handoffs. A teaser can name an object or outcome while preserving another worthwhile discovery. Develop interest during a delay, and let the ending answer the actual promise. A preview, joke, aside or reflection can help. Improve its particular effect rather than deleting the device or calling a solemn warning a joke.

Use `duration_context` to compare the current estimate with the assignment's target and range. Choose the needed scale of change, allowing natural variation in delivery. A complete shorter telling can require removing a detour, rather than compressing every existing point. Make room for an essential addition through selection elsewhere.

## Write and verify the new telling

Use idiomatic speech, concrete images, active verbs and varied cadence suited to the assigned voices. Let contributions change responses in dialogue, keeping knowledge plausible for each role. Preserve presence, feeling, humor and breathing room as well as the answer. Reflection should add a particular thought about the subject. Rebuttals of claims the account never makes and declarations of the author's responsibility do not substitute for that work.

Check proposed factual corrections against the supplied evidence. A review identifies a suspected problem; it does not establish a new fact. Resolve the actual misleading claim through wording, scope, timing or attribution, or remove a dispensable thought. Keep retained quantities accurate. Source cautions need not become narration: accurate attribution or framing may suffice. Add explanation when the retained account still needs it or the client requires it. Preserve uncertainty that changes its meaning. After moving passages, recheck dates, causal links and who knew, observed or reported each retained fact. Reconsider the resulting composition: an accurate reorder can spend a discovery early or leave a scene without a purpose. Let listeners connect facts when the connection is already clear.

Scenic reconstruction, original illustrations and imaginative texture may enliven the telling within the brief. They cannot create substantive evidence, change established events or present invented words as source testimony. Preserve supplied source quotations exactly.
