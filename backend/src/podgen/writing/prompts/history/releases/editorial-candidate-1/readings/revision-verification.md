# Edit the revised episode

Read the complete current script for its audience. Hear what changes from the opening to the last line. Would you record this version?

Then read it against the previous script and the assignment the writer received. In previous_script_in_order, copy_current repeats the current turns from first to last inclusive; previous_turns supplies the old spoken words where they differ. Old anchors refer to that reconstructed script. R identifies reader assignments and F identifies factual assignments. The fact checker independently verifies the sources.

For each assignment, say what the listener hears differently and whether that solves the underlying problem. A better solution may use different words, a different scene or a cut. A changed story can also make a request unnecessary. Compare the whole episode: an edit can fix a line and still cost the story a discovery, a laugh or a convincing ending. Keep what works. If anything still needs attention, commission the actual repair on the current script and refer to its finding index from the outstanding assignment. Separate new problems from the old ones. Use the scope that gives the writer room to do the work. Use output_language.

## Worked edits

**A listener needs one piece of shop knowledge.**
- Draft: “On the poster the cyclist's red cap floated an inch above her head. Inez laid the colour stencils over their pins. Two holes keep each stencil in place. If a hole gets wider, the stencil can move. Moving the stencil moves the colour. The left hole in the red stencil had torn into an oval.”
- Problem: The pins are new information. After that, the torn hole shows what went wrong.
- Assignment: Local. Keep how the stencils are held and what Inez finds. Cut the intervening lesson.
- Edited: “On the poster the cyclist's red cap floated an inch above her head. Inez laid the colour stencils over their pins. Two holes keep each stencil in place. The left hole in the red stencil had torn into an oval.”

**A dry line has done its job.**
- Draft: “Nora tried to open her new scissors. She pulled at the plastic. She tried her teeth. ‘I know a tool for this,’ she said. She meant the scissors inside the package.”
- Problem: We can see what Nora can see. The following explanation takes the laugh away from her.
- Assignment: Local. Keep the struggle and Nora's own line exactly; end there.
- Edited: “Nora tried to open her new scissors. She pulled at the plastic. She tried her teeth. ‘I know a tool for this,’ she said.”

**The answer arrives before anyone has a reason to want it.**
- Draft: “An old register identified the boy as Nico Vera. In Kit's classroom photograph every child had a name except the one with the drum. Kit had tried matching his face to other photographs. A builder later found the school band register behind a wall. It listed the musicians by instrument. There was a single entry for drum, Nico Vera. So the search was successful. Photographs and records work well together.”
- Problem: Giving the name away makes the search idle. Cutting only the final summary would still leave the reveal in the wrong place.
- Assignment: Sequence, from the first mention of the register through the close. Give Kit her unanswered question, keep the failed search by face, then let the recovered register change how we look. Compose the last beat from its actual entry.
- Edited: “In Kit's classroom photograph, only the boy with the drum had no name. She tried matching his face to other photographs. Then a builder found the school band register behind a wall. The musicians were listed by instrument. This time Kit didn't need to recognize his face. Under ‘Drum’ there was one name: Nico Vera.”
