# Listen to the episode in playback order

Decide whether you would give this script to the assigned speakers to record. Read only its words, the genre, format and stated audience. Report `reading_mode=script_only`. Use `output_language` for prose. Preserve speaker labels, quotes and keys exactly.

## Follow one listening

At each decisive action, stop before the next sentence. From the words heard so far, what would this audience think prompted the action? How does the reported observation bear on the person's question? For an explanation, ask which premise lets a first-time listener make the connection. If your answer relies on your own subject knowledge, locate the gap in the speech. Ask for the specific missing connection or a different entry. An intended mystery leaves the answer open while giving listeners a reason to care. If the missing basis makes the central action or question arbitrary, require a repair even when the ending eventually makes sense.

Make two paper edits using only the supplied speech. Give the actual cut you would send to recording. For the last passage, try ending at an earlier discovery or reflection and try joining it directly to the last words you would keep. What, specifically, does the intervening speech let this audience experience after it already has the answer? Decide the last line or image and what should lead into it. If the original delivers that moment best, keep it. If the alternate cut makes the better finished episode, request it as a required edit. Distinguish the contribution of a last image from that of the speech immediately before it; a strong image can survive removing a weak lead-in.

At the forward-looking handoff that makes the largest promise, play the last line before the cut directly into the first material that pays it off. What did the listener expect, and what did the next scene actually give them? Decide which wording and sequence you would air. A successful tease may stay; a later scene may deserve to stay even if its announcement needs to go. When there is no such handoff, omit this decision.

Report a decision to change in the ordinary findings and revision scope; report a decision to keep in strengths. Quote the real words on both sides of each cut and give the practical reason. Give the rewriter permission for the cut you actually want. When a cut removes a connected group of complete utterances or rebuilds a sequence, choose sequence scope and mark its inclusive first and last utterances with two short exact boundary quotes in sequence_ranges. A range may start and end in the same utterance. A local edit can change only the quoted wording: for a local deletion, quote the entire passage to delete. Quotes of the words you want to keep identify the join but do not authorize deleting the speech between them. Do not give a second version of the script. Follow discoveries inside scenes too: once a connection has been disclosed, what still remains worth hearing?

## Hear the wording

Hear the actual words as spoken, including case, agreement, idiom and cadence. An objective grammatical error requires an `important` finding and local correction. Distinguish it from a speaker's deliberate conversational phrasing. Around an action, mentally remove the surrounding phrases and hear what disappears: a new thought, a concrete image, a joke, tension, needed orientation, or one more description of what the listener already knows? Attend to the combined effect of nearby lines. A figurative phrase is not a defect by itself. In dialogue, judge the exchange in the context of the speakers' roles.

Notice a narrator instructing the audience how to react, discussing how responsibly the account is being told, or rebutting a claim nobody has made. Quote the actual implication when a qualification is needed. Keep pleasure, attitude, vivid detail, a well-timed aside or suspense when the words deliver them.

## Give an actionable decision

If you would accept the passage for recording only after this change, use `important` or `blocking` and recommend `revise`. Use `optional` for alternatives equally acceptable for recording. Use `keep` when no required repair remains. Consider the listening effect, independently of how short the edit would be. Praise for a story choice can coexist with a required change to its wording.

Anchor each finding with exact nonempty quotations and supplied zero-based utterance indices. Name what the listener heard, what it does to this particular passage, the editorial operation and what should survive. Anchor a missing premise to the words that depend on it. For a failed promise or repeated ending, address both the setup and the later passage. Choose local, sequence or whole-episode scope according to what must change. Use `duration_context` as an estimate when considering pace and selection. Leave `research_requests` empty. External verification belongs to the evidence reading; do not claim measured audio or human listening.
