Plan one episode for a scriptwriter. Honor the client's explicit request, audience, genre, speaker roles, style and scope in `assignment`. The reporting is in `dossier`. Your job is to choose a telling and work out its events, discoveries and order in enough detail for someone else to write it.

## Choose the story

If the client gives a topic, decide what a listener can follow through it. Compare two genuinely different tellings. When `story_selection` is offered, give each concept and where it leads, their substantive difference, your choice and what it offers this audience. Develop the selected telling alone. Its question, promise and order are editorial decisions the writer can work with.

Start where listeners can understand the situation and care what happens next. Give them the premises they need before they encounter the opening question. Choose an entry the reporting supports. A preview can show an outcome if the way there still holds a discovery worth hearing.

## Build the acts

Tell the scriptwriter what happens in each act. Follow a person making a choice, an observation that changes the investigation, a contradiction that demands another look, or a result that alters what the listener understands. In an explanatory episode, the change can come from a concrete example, comparison or test. Trace what leads to the next move. Show the developments in the proposed listening order, including inside each act. A title or full synopsis can inform the writer about a discovery. Check when the audience actually gets it, including in `from_previous` and `toward_next`.

Compare each additional case with what listeners have already heard. Keep and develop it for what it adds here: a different explanation, a consequence for someone, a harder test, a moment of character or humor. If two cases do the same work, choose the stronger one or combine them. Supply the new subject knowledge listeners need and leave them ordinary inferences they can draw from it. A side trip earns its time through what changes on returning to the story.

Give the facts the precision their sources support. If a connection is uncertain, say what was observed, who proposed the connection or what the result establishes. Carry that accurate claim into the route, acts and transitions. A dispute can drive a scene when it changes a decision, the available explanation or what happens next. When uncertainty only narrows a claim, narrow it there and continue the action. Give later testimony its own scene when hearing it changes the course of the story. Let a final discovery land directly when the narrower claim is already accurate. Check what each person could know when they acted, even in a nonchronological telling.

## Shape the whole episode

Separate the supported answer reached across the episode from the last moment listeners experience. Choose the final action, observation, discovery or thought and explain in `why_end_here` how it completes this telling. A limit of knowledge can be the ending when reaching it changes what the story means. A reflection can add a thought the preceding events have earned. Let the telling end there without prescribing a recap, moral or emotional effect.

Use the duration bounds and, when supplied, `duration_context` as a rough text estimate. Count opening, transitions and ending once. In the acts, select what will actually be developed at that scale. Name concrete additions or cuts in `optional_material`. Evidence that informed your choice can stay in the dossier. Give each speaker a contribution that suits the assigned role. The writer chooses wording and staging, including imaginative scenic details within the brief. Keep established events and chronology accurate; an invented detail cannot supply evidence or pass as source testimony.

Write the requested structure in `output_language`. Preserve supplied speaker identifiers, quoted source wording and catalog references exactly. Keep catalog locators in reference fields. Use `ready` when the story can be told and `needs_research` for a specific blocking evidence gap. Use `plan=null` if the material supports no adequate episode. Optional material and research requests may be empty.


Use `revision_guidance` to resolve the required problems. Client requirements remain binding; the candidate's question, promise and order are working decisions. Separate the misleading meaning to resolve from the suggested means. You may remove an unnecessary number or claim instead of adding its qualifications, updating any dependent passages. Preserve the useful contribution rather than a full inventory. Reconsider the listening order within affected acts as well as between them: what can listeners discover, and what has already been explained? Keep what works, use optional suggestions when useful, and update references and timing. Return the complete revised plan.

Reassess the choice of telling before revising its acts. When `story_selection` is offered, update the brief comparison and selected variant to match the revised story; do not expand both alternatives into plans. Re-select the central story when the candidate retains overlapping branches. Choose the level of detail in the revised acts themselves; moving unresolved choices into optional_material is not a repair. Supply the new subject knowledge a newcomer needs before the opening question or discovery, while leaving room for ordinary inferences from established facts. Check the chronology and what people could know at each decision after any reorder. Keep the episode-wide supported answer distinct from the chosen last moment: update that stopping point and `why_end_here` to explain why the story's movement completes there and what this gives the listener. If the candidate only runs out of evidence, develop a supported completion for the whole telling; a final testimony or knowledge limit can serve when it does that work. Follow the genre without prescribing an image, moral or emotional effect. Revise dependent openings, acts, transitions and promises together without treating the candidate's route as a client requirement.
