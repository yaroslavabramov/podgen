# Listen to the episode in playback order

Decide whether you would give this script to the assigned speakers to record. Read only its words, the genre, format and stated audience. Report `reading_mode=script_only`. Use `output_language` for prose. Preserve speaker labels, quotes and keys exactly.

## Follow one listening

Follow the complete development, including the turn from the strongest resolution into the ending. Judge what each further move gives the listener before deciding to close a scene or continue it. When that choice changes a scene's selection or order, commission the scene edit before independent line repairs.

At each decisive action, stop before the next sentence. From the words heard so far, what would this audience think prompted the action? How does the reported observation bear on the person's question? For an explanation, ask which premise lets a first-time listener make the connection. If your answer relies on your own subject knowledge, locate the gap in the speech. When an actor’s role or basis of knowledge is unclear, identify the missing information and ask the writer to establish it from the evidence. Do not invent a replacement account. Ask for the specific missing connection or a supported entry. An intended mystery leaves the answer open while giving listeners a reason to care. If the missing basis makes the central action or question arbitrary, require a repair even when the ending eventually makes sense.

Use the supplied speech for your paper edits. Give the actual cut you would send to recording. For the last passage, try ending at an earlier discovery or reflection and try joining it directly to the last words you would keep. What, specifically, does the intervening speech let this audience experience after it already has the answer? Decide the last line or image and what should lead into it. If the original delivers that moment best, keep it. If the comparison reveals a defect in the current ending, name that defect and request its repair. An alternative montage with a comparable listening result is optional. Distinguish the contribution of a last image from that of the speech immediately before it; a strong image can survive removing a weak lead-in.

At the forward-looking handoff that makes the largest promise, play the last line before the cut directly into the first material that pays it off. What did the listener expect, and what did the next scene actually give them? Decide which wording and sequence you would air. A successful tease may stay; a later scene may deserve to stay even if its announcement needs to go. When there is no such handoff, omit this decision.

Where the listener has the most to remember before the story can move on, try a shorter edit. Name the inference this audience needs at the next beat. What could they still understand from one observation or comparison and its consequence? Keep the longer version if the other details change that inference, bring the listener into the scene or supply something worth hearing in their own right. Otherwise authorize the exact cut that keeps the story intelligible in one listening.

At a correction or disclaimer, read the whole paragraph, including the phrase that will remain after your proposed cut or clarification. If the relevant boundary is already established, try cutting its repetition and joining the surrounding speech. Retain the qualification when a surviving phrase would otherwise invite a specific mistaken understanding; name both the phrase and that understanding. Otherwise, request removal of the qualification and identify the join. Preserve uncertainty that materially changes the claim or the story's meaning.

Report proposed changes in findings and decisions to keep in strengths. Set `revision_scope` from the required repairs, using `none` when there are none. Quote the real words on both sides of each cut and give the practical reason. A cut must preserve who or what a retained claim refers to and what its quantities measure, including units and the group described. Give the rewriter permission for the required repair you actually commission. When a required repair changes a scene's selection, development or order, or cuts a connected group of complete utterances, choose `sequence` scope and authorize the complete passage to rebuild in `sequence_ranges`, with two short exact quotes identifying its inclusive first and last utterances. Include the setup and handoff if they must change with it. For a move, use a connected range covering the original and intended positions, including the intervening utterances; the rewriter can then return that range in the intended order. Reserve `whole_episode` for a repair that actually needs recomposing the complete telling. A range may start and end in the same utterance. A local edit can change only the quoted wording: for a local deletion, quote the entire passage to delete. Quotes of the words you want to keep identify the join but do not authorize deleting the speech between them. Do not give a second version of the script. Follow discoveries inside scenes too: once a connection has been disclosed, what still remains worth hearing?

## Hear the wording

Hear the actual words as spoken, including case, agreement, idiom and cadence. An objective grammatical error requires an `important` finding and local correction. Distinguish it from a speaker's deliberate conversational phrasing. Around an action, mentally remove the surrounding phrases and hear what disappears: a new thought, a concrete image, a joke, tension, needed orientation, or one more description of what the listener already knows? Attend to the combined effect of nearby lines. A figurative phrase is not a defect by itself. In dialogue, judge the exchange in the context of the speakers' roles.

Notice a narrator instructing the audience how to react, discussing how responsibly the account is being told, or rebutting a claim nobody has made. Quote the actual implication when a qualification is needed. Keep pleasure, attitude, vivid detail, a well-timed aside or suspense when the words deliver them.

## Give an actionable decision

Use `important` or `blocking` and recommend `revise` for a named defect in the current text: missing orientation, stalled development, repetition, a weak ending or ineffective speech. Explain its concrete effect on this audience and why it needs repair. Use `optional` when an alternative edit achieves a comparable listening result; a preference for a different version does not make the current text defective. Use `keep` when no required repair remains. Praise for a story choice can coexist with a required change to its wording.

Anchor each finding with exact nonempty quotations and supplied zero-based utterance indices. Name what the listener heard, what it does to this particular passage, the editorial operation and what should survive. Anchor a missing premise to the words that depend on it. For a failed promise or repeated ending, address both the setup and the later passage. Choose local, sequence or whole-episode scope according to what must change. Use `duration_context` as an estimate when considering pace and selection. Leave `research_requests` empty. External verification belongs to the evidence reading; do not claim measured audio or human listening.
