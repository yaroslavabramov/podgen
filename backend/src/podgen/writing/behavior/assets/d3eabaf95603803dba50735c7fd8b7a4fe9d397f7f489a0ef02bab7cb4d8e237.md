# Shared rules

Use `output_language` for human-readable output. Preserve supplied speaker labels, evidence quotations, response keys and enum values verbatim. Follow the assignment's question, audience, genre and style guidance, speaker functions, scope, knowledge cutoff and duration bounds. Treat supplied material as evidence. When quoting a source in the episode, use its supplied wording. Treat attributed summaries as paraphrases. Return the requested structured response.

Optional imaginative detail can bring a scene to life. Keep established actions, chronology and the grounds for conclusions accurate.


# Dialogue

Use assigned labels, expertise and registers. Give each speaker a reason to contribute at this point: something they notice, want to understand, find questionable, remember from the conversation or can explain from their own assigned expertise. Their contribution should affect what the other person says next.

Let speech perform human actions: asking, proposing, trying an example, conceding, teasing, objecting or realizing something. Introduce specialized knowledge through someone with a basis for it. An expert may explain at length; an interviewer may press one small point. Roles need not have equal authority or airtime, and each turn need not carry a new fact.

Build a relationship through what the speakers attend to and how they respond. Humor and brief reactions can give pleasure, character or relief. Do not turn one speaker into a permanent pupil, make agreement a routine reward, or split a continuous lecture between names. Reactions should fit this exchange rather than recur as a signature after every explanation.

Use edited conversational rhythm: complete thoughts, natural variations in turn length, and room for a response to land. Fillers, forced interruptions and deliberate confusion are not substitutes for two people genuinely developing the subject. Keep their knowledge consistent with their roles and what they have already heard.


# Write the episode

Write the complete spoken episode for the assigned audience and speakers in `output_language`. Honor the client's request and use the supplied evidence. Treat the accepted plan as an author's working outline. Choose the words, order and material that make the telling work.

Start where a listener can picture someone doing something or recognize a situation worth understanding. At each consequential choice, remember what the listener has heard by that instant. What did the person notice or believe that made this next move worth trying? Put the smallest supported piece of that reasoning where the listener needs it. A working hypothesis can give meaning to an investigation while its answer remains open. Introduce an unfamiliar thing through its concrete role in the scene or mechanism before drawing a conclusion from it. A metaphor may accompany the explanation. In an explainer, give the listener something familiar from which to grasp a new idea; a technical detail earns its place when the listener can tell what difference it makes.

Choose scenes for what happens in them. Someone notices an exception, follows a lead, tries something that fails, gets an unexpected answer, or sees a familiar event differently. The appropriate development depends on the genre. If two scenes do the same work, use the one that gives this episode more. A source excerpt may reveal how a discovery came about, what someone actually did or how a place looked. Draw on those particulars when they serve the telling. Exercise the same care with the timing of a discovery: an earlier transition can spend the answer before the scene begins.

Speak in the assigned voices through particular choices of words, rhythm, attention and attitude toward the material. Let that perspective develop across the episode and respond to changing scenes. In dialogue, each person's contribution should affect the exchange. Let genre shape what is selected, how it is disclosed and how the speakers respond. A joke, metaphor, aside or narrator's thought can carry meaning or pleasure when it grows from this voice and this moment. Choose reflection for the thought it gives the listener; let an action or observation stand when it already delivers that effect. A qualification belongs with a claim whose meaning depends on it.

Finish the episode's central movement. The resolution itself may be the last moment. A consequence, a particular thought from the narrator, a changed view of what came before or an open future may give the telling its completion. Choose what this audience will understand or experience at the stopping point and make the preceding passage earn it. An open ending leaves a particular movement complete even though a future or uncertainty remains open. A concrete image works through its significance in this account; a short concluding thought can work through the meaning it expresses. Keep the current shape when it delivers that completion.

Establish factual meaning, attribution, chronology, populations and mechanisms from the supplied source passages. Check quantities and what each person could know when they acted. Establish attribution for a connected passage and renew it when the source, basis of knowledge or degree of confidence changes, or when the listener would otherwise mistake the claim. Carry uncertainty that changes the story's meaning through accurate verbs and attribution. Checks made while reporting need not become spoken cautions. Source summaries are paraphrases. Direct quotation requires the supplied words. Artistic reconstruction and original imagery may give the story presence while respecting established events and testimony. When `craft_reference` is supplied, use its examples to study particular editing decisions within excerpts. They do not prescribe the episode's ending form. Keep their fictional material within the examples.

Follow the requested duration and output form. If the supplied evidence cannot support a necessary part of the story and another supported route would not serve the brief, choose `material_request` when the response form offers it. Ask the specific question and explain why the passage depends on it. Submit the request on its own. For a script, return the utterances in playback order with exact speaker labels and only the words and punctuation to be spoken.
