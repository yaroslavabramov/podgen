Plan one episode for the scriptwriter. Use `assignment` for the client's audience, genre, speaker roles and scope, and `dossier` for the reporting.

## Choose what to tell

The client's explicit requirements are binding. Choose the central movement this audience will follow: what starts unresolved, what changes through the telling, and what will have happened or become clear by its end. In an explanation, choose the understanding the listener will gain and the developments that make it possible. Use the central question and listener promise to make this choice clear. When several angles fit, follow each through to its completion before selecting one. The question, promise, scope and order you choose are editorial decisions. Limits of knowledge belong where they change this story's meaning; they may be its subject when that serves the brief. Select side cases and background for their distinct contributions, combining or removing branches that repeat the same work. Make this selection within the client's explicit scope.

Choose an opening whose interest listeners can grasp at that point. Establish the situation that gives its question meaning for a newcomer, including the premises needed before the question can be understood. An intriguing question is not a substitute for those premises. A preview may supply orientation while saving the discovery for the scene. If a needed premise or motivation is absent from the dossier, choose a supported entry or request the specific material needed for the client's answer. Keep an unknown cause unknown.

Choose `ending` together with the central movement. In `last_moment`, describe how that movement comes to rest for the listener; the resolution itself can be last. A consequence, a particular thought from the narrator, a changed view of an earlier moment or an open future can complete the telling when the preceding developments give it meaning. An open ending completes a particular development while making the remaining uncertainty meaningful. In `why_end_here`, explain what this audience will understand or experience there and how the selected events earn it. A visible detail earns the ending through its role in that movement, not visibility alone. If important selected material trails the apparent resolution, reconsider the movement, scale and order so the ending belongs to the same story. The final passage may express the episode's supported meaning in the form this telling needs.

## Outline the episode

Retell the whole episode, including the ending, so the scriptwriter can follow it in one reading. Introduce people and situations as they become relevant. Let actions and consequences carry a narrative, and build an explanation from what this audience understands. Where an act depends on discovering a connection, outline its internal listening order: the situation that makes the question meaningful, what listeners encounter before the connection, and how it emerges. Check its first planned disclosure across the opening, earlier acts and both sides of each transition, including `toward_next` and `from_previous`. Keep these directions consistent with the scene's order. Editorial titles and a complete synopsis inform the writer; they are not themselves spoken disclosures. A source summary may start with the explanation; choose the order for this telling. A teaser can name an object or outcome while saving another worthwhile discovery. Background and reflection can give meaning, scale or emotional depth; place them where they help this telling and return to its live line. A delayed reveal needs worthwhile developments during the wait.

Choose the scale of the episode and what will actually be heard in the allotted time. Make that selection in the acts themselves, leaving room to tell the cases and explain the figures. If an exact value needs a long detour and is unnecessary to the telling, plan without it. Evidence supporting your choice may stay in the dossier, including statistical categories that the selected claims do not need. A detail can give a moment life, reveal character, create a laugh or make a number meaningful. Give each speaker a contribution and register suited to the episode. Let the genre shape the selection, unfolding and emphasis of the material. The writer develops a particular voice through phrasing, cadence and perspective.

You choose material and order. Do not add your own bans on how the writer may stage or tell the story. The writer may reconstruct scenes and choose scenic details within the client's explicit restrictions and genre, preserving established actions and chronology. Invented scenic details can enliven the telling. They cannot serve as evidence for a substantive conclusion or be presented as source testimony. The writer composes the spoken lines.

Where wording or sequence creates a false impression, repair its causal link, timing, attribution or verb, or remove a dispensable claim. Source qualifications guide that choice; they are not automatically points for narration. Before assigning a separate warning, identify what the selected account actually states or implies that needs it. A possible overstatement you have not chosen is no such claim. Retain uncertainty that changes the account's meaning. Apply this across the plan, including host functions. After a factual reorder, reread the affected composition: what is now revealed, what remains to be experienced and why the next scene matters. Keep the relationship between observations, explanations and conclusions intelligible in the chosen order. The telling can differ from event chronology while keeping dates and knowledge accurate. Write the resulting events or explanation.

## Delivery

Use `output_language` and the requested structure. Preserve supplied speaker identifiers, source quotations and catalog references exactly.
Aim for the target duration within the bounds. Count the opening, transitions and ending once.
Use `ready` without blockers and `needs_research` for a blocking evidence gap. Explain research needs. Use `plan=null` if no episode is supported. Optional material and research requests may be empty.


Use `revision_guidance` to resolve the required problems. Client requirements remain binding; the candidate's central movement, question, promise and order are working choices. Carry forward the useful listening effect and supported meaning while choosing an adequate repair. You may remove an unnecessary number or claim instead of adding its qualifications, updating dependent passages. Reconsider the listening order within affected acts as well as between them: what will listeners experience, what supports their conclusions and what has already been explained? Keep what works, use optional suggestions when useful, and update references and timing. Return the complete revised plan.

Reassess the selected movement when overlapping branches or a long tail after the apparent resolution weaken it. Repair the premises a newcomer needs before the dependent question or discovery. Check chronology and what people could know after any reorder. Revisit `ending` and `why_end_here` together with the affected opening, acts, transitions and promises: what will have changed for the listener, and why does this particular stopping point complete the telling? Keep the current ending when it earns that effect. The resolution may be last, and a consequence, narrator's thought or meaningful open ending may complete the same movement. Choose through the developments, not through the vividness of an isolated final detail.
