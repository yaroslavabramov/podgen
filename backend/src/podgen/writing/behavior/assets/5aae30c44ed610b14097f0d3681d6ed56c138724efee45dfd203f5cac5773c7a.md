# Revise the spoken episode

Read the current script as a listener, then use both independent reviews to revise it in `output_language`. Honor the client's question, format, speakers and duration. The editorial frame describes working choices. A finding identifies a problem and may suggest one remedy. Decide what the passage needs to accomplish in the episode you are actually hearing.

Work at the scale of the trouble. A series of individually accurate lines can stall an investigation or turn a conversation into a lesson. For a scene assignment, rebuild the offered passage to deliver the change the editor wants the listener to experience. Keep the part of a detail that earns its place there: a reason to act, a revealing observation, an uncertainty that changes what we can conclude, a beat that gets a laugh. When moving a passage, hear how much of it the new scene needs. Read both joins with the untouched speech.

Trace an unclear move back to the specific fact, working belief or mechanism the listener is missing. Supply that beside the move. Once the facts invite a straightforward inference, move on to what follows from it. In a factual repair, check what the replacement itself claims. A detail borrowed from elsewhere must still describe the same people, objects and relationship it describes in the evidence. Hear what your new image implies as well as what it literally says. If a precise detail is unnecessary, the observed action or image can carry the line on its own. Preserve the timing of discoveries.

Hear what a narrator is doing in context. A correction that sounds like an aside may be a dry joke, a genuine clarification or an author defending the telling. Judge the setup, phrasing and timing on the page. Keep or sharpen a line whose delivery gives the scene character. When precision matters, repair the claim itself. If the voice has become defensive over several lines, rewrite the passage in the narrator's register.

Listen through the last scene. An earlier stopping point can already complete the story. If cutting the recap leaves an abrupt stop, write a new last beat from the supported material. Let the final action, image or thought show what has changed for the listener. Uncertainty can be the point when the scene gives it consequence. A further account of the episode's answer can spend an ending that already works.

Use the operation the response permits. In `targeted` mode, choose the offered text or insertion boundary your repair needs. In `sequence` mode, return the complete new spoken turns for each range you replace; that can include newly written speech, a different number of turns, or an empty range for a cut. If the range contains the ending, you can compose a new final scene there. In a whole-script revision, shape the complete telling. Respect the scope, speaker labels, source wording, factual meaning and chronology. Keep only spoken words and punctuation in utterance `text`.

If a required repair genuinely depends on missing evidence and another supported route cannot serve the brief, use `material_request` when offered. Ask the specific question and explain what depends on it. When present, draw on `craft_reference` for editorial reasoning.
