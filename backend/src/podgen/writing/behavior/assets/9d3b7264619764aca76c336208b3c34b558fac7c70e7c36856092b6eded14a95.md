# Verify the episode's contextual claims

Check the complete supplied script against its evidence and the explicit client request in `factual_remit`. Report `reading_mode=informed`. Use `output_language` for prose, preserving speaker labels, source labels, quotations, identifiers and response keys verbatim. Assess this version's factual account, whether it is a draft or a final.

## Establish the claim before checking it

Read the connected passage and the conversation around it. Establish who or what the statement concerns, its time and scope, whose knowledge or explanation is being reported, and what the listener would reasonably understand. Resolve references, conversational shorthand and attribution from that context. Read exceptions and qualifications already present in the script with the claim they affect. In `listener_answer`, reconstruct the material assertions at that contextual scope. A possible isolated literal reading is a reason to inspect the passage, not yet a factual finding. A temporal contrast establishes the difference relevant to its passage, rather than an inventory of every observation behind an earlier decision. Require more history only when the contextual account would otherwise be false.

Independently verify precise details: identities and names, dates, quantities, units and calculations. Match names, including given names and transliterations, to the supplied passages or bibliographic information. Check what an exact date dates and what a quantity counts, over which period and population. Judge an approximation at the precision claimed. A plausible story does not confirm a precise detail; when the supplied material does not establish it, identify the missing support rather than completing it from memory.

## Compare the claim with the evidence

Find the relevant source passages and attributed reporting and read them together. Distinguish observation, testimony, hypothesis and inference, and independent support from repeated accounts. Check what each person could know when acting. A later or secondhand report can coexist with an earlier account. Researcher limitations and other research notes are pointers for this comparison: establish what the evidence actually supports for the spoken claim. In `important_limits`, record limits that affect that assessment; this private comparison may be empty and is not a narration assignment. For a causal statement, consider its contextual scope, established attribution and actual evidential support. The possibility of another cause alone does not establish a contradiction.

Distinguish the basis of a potential finding in `observation`:

- A contradiction: the evidence establishes something incompatible with the claim as heard in context. Identify the incompatibility.
- An unsupported material detail: the script adds a concrete assertion the supplied material does not establish. Identify the addition and the support missing; absence of confirmation does not establish a contrary account.
- A genuine ambiguity: the surrounding passage still permits different plausible factual readings. Identify them, the words that leave them open and how they change the factual understanding.

Then justify the need for repair separately. Use `important` or `blocking` when the discrepancy would mislead this audience about an identity, event, scope, attribution, confidence, mechanism or causal conclusion. Explain that consequence in this account. An unsupported independent factual assertion needs correction or removal even if incidental to the main argument; dispensability does not make it an optional accuracy improvement. The mandatory status must follow from the contextual claim and evidence comparison, not from a merely imaginable reading, a general preference for caution or the presence of a research limitation. Use `optional` for a concrete improvement that leaves an already adequate factual account equally acceptable. Omit a finding when there is no useful change to propose.

Optional scenic reconstruction can add presence while keeping established actions, chronology and grounds for conclusions accurate. Assess whether it changes a factual claim or supplies unsupported evidence for a conclusion. Check direct quotations against the supplied words and distinguish them from paraphrases.

## Give the writer an adequate repair

In every `requested_change`, including an optional one, choose one concrete correction and state the supported meaning the result should convey. Correct an identifying detail, narrow a claim, attribute an explanation or remove an unnecessary assertion as the evidence and telling require. The writer may choose the spoken wording and may remove a dispensable phrase together with its dependent wording instead of adding qualifications. Follow the effect into dependent claims, quantities and transitions. A removed claim does not need a spoken caveat. Require an explanation only when the surviving account needs it for accuracy or understanding, or the client explicitly requires it.

In `preserve`, name the essential supported meaning, factual conditions and useful contribution to retain. Preserve uncertainty when it changes that meaning. A current number, subgroup, phrase or warning can change or disappear while its useful contribution survives. Anchor the actual problem to exact nonempty substrings and supplied zero-based utterance indices; for an omission, anchor the words that depend on the missing information. Cite the relevant source passages and copy their offered `support_labels` exactly.

## Return a consistent decision

Recommend `revise` when a required factual repair can be made from the supplied material. Use `needs_material` only for a specific evidence gap that prevents an adequate version within scope; anchor the request and state what must be established and why the passage needs it. A supported bounded account may suffice. Recommend `keep` when no required factual repair or blocking evidence gap remains, including when optional suggestions remain. With `keep`, set `revision_scope.scale` to `none`; the scope describes required revision.

Return only the requested structured review. This is an evidence assessment, with no claim of human listening or measured audio. For cards with `card_id`, return only their exact offered IDs in `support_labels`; `label` and `title` are display names and may repeat. For historical cards without `card_id`, use their offered label keys.
