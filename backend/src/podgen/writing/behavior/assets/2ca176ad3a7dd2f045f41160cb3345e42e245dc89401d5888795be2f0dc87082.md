# Revise the spoken episode

Deliver the revision in `output_language`. Read the actual words and both independent reviews. Honor the client's question, format and speakers. Use `editorial_frame` as the current working composition. If `accepted_plan` is supplied separately, it records the admitted starting plan; later explicit composition decisions govern the current arrangement. Work from the listener's experience of this version.

Resolve the problem underneath each required finding. A reviewer's suggested remedy is one possible edit. An anchored strength tells you what contribution to carry forward while changing the words or order. For optional suggestions, decide what the particular passage gains. Shape both ends of each change so it belongs to the surviving text. Treat `preserve` as the contribution or supported meaning to retain, not an obligation to repeat the old wording or add an explanatory line. Keep a qualification beside any retained claim that depends on it.

When a listener cannot follow a choice or explanation, read up to that point and establish the observation, working belief or mechanism that makes the next move intelligible. Introduce an unfamiliar thing through its concrete role in the scene or mechanism before drawing a conclusion from it; a metaphor may accompany the explanation. Put the necessary support beside the dependent move in ordinary speech. A genuine question that the story later answers can remain open. An unexplained reason the audience needs just to recognize that question calls for a repair now. Use the supplied evidence, including relevant source details. If a required repair depends on missing evidence and an alternative route would not serve the brief, choose `material_request` when the response form offers it. Ask the specific question, say why that passage depends on it, and submit the request on its own.

Edit the sequence as well as the sentences. Check what each episode contributes after the preceding one. Merge, move or remove repeated work. At a tease or delayed disclosure, locate what the listener already knows and what the later scene actually delivers. Restore an early-spent discovery at the first words which disclose it. If the answer is already clear before the closing passage, choose a last action, image or thought that adds something; cut the extra wrap-up. Retain callbacks, humor, reflection and useful orientation where they earn their place.

Follow the supplied `revision_operation.mode` and its editable targets or ranges. In targeted mode choose replacement, deletion or insertion within the allowed locations. In sequence mode, return the complete replacement `utterances` for each edited `range_id`, including everything to keep within it in the intended order. Rebuild the scene's development and connections rather than limiting yourself to substitutions at the quoted problems; you may merge, move or remove material inside the offered range. Preserve speech outside the offered locations and respect the requested output form. Fit duration through selection and pacing. If extra explanation is needed, consider where a cut can make room. An accurate but dispensable number and its dependent comparisons can be removed together.

Give each speaker an idiomatic, audible voice. Locate commentary on the telling itself: announcing a genre, certifying the narrator's care, rebutting an unmade claim, or telling listeners how impressed to be. Where it merely repeats the work of the scene, let a particular observation, action or line of thought carry that moment. A distinct voice, aside or joke can deserve to stay.

Neither review is a source of historical facts. Verify proposed changes to attribution, chronology, populations and mechanisms against the supplied source passages, regardless of which reviewer requested them. When a suggested remedy contradicts the evidence, resolve the reported problem while retaining the supported account. Check any direct quotation against its supplied wording. Use a more precise statement, attribution, placement or cut as the passage requires. State the corrected account directly; the superseded draft is not a claim the speakers need to refute. Preserve chronology and who knew what when; recheck dependent quantities. Scenic reconstruction can add color while respecting events and testimony. When `craft_reference` is present, draw on its editorial choices, keeping its fictional material out of the episode.

Return the edits or complete revision permitted by the response form when the evidence suffices. Use exact speaker labels and only spoken words and punctuation in utterance `text`.
