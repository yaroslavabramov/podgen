# Why the writing workflow was rebuilt

September 2026 design decision. This review concerns the two prototype backends preserved in the [legacy snapshot](README.md).

## What the prototypes revealed

The first prototype assigned facts to scenes before drafting. A scene writer could satisfy the local constraints and still leave a listener without the connection between two ideas. The same facts could then be explained again in the conclusion. Counting unused facts and repeated identifiers failed to distinguish a useful reminder from a second explanation.

The second prototype gave a researcher tools to explore a source corpus. Three recorded runs on the issuance of Hong Kong banknotes used 80, 74 and 80 tool rounds respectively. None registered a finding, unresolved question or completed assignment. This happened with two model profiles. The result exposed a flaw in the working environment: the researcher could keep reading without delivering a usable account to the writer.

Both approaches accumulated records of work without giving an editor a reliable view of what the listener had already learned. Local corrections to a script were rarely tested by reading the revised episode from the beginning.

## Decision

Keep the original source, its extracted representation and stable text ranges. Let research produce a coherent account with supporting evidence, open questions and competing interpretations. The planner selects the story and decides what to leave out. The writer receives the accepted plan and can ask for missing evidence. The editor reads the whole script; the fact-checker can return to the sources behind individual claims.

Give one writer responsibility for the episode's overall movement. Break work into acts only when it improves the resulting script in a comparison on the same material. A fact may appear twice if its second use changes what the listener understands.

## How to test the decision

Use a mix of historical narratives, mechanisms, disputed claims, sparse evidence and large uneven corpora. Keep some topics outside prompt development. Compare the proposed workflow with one strong model using simple tools, the same corpus and the same final checks. Evaluate the opening promise, comprehension, substantive factual errors and listening experience. Keep changes to source access separate from changes to writing behavior so a better dossier cannot masquerade as a better writer.
